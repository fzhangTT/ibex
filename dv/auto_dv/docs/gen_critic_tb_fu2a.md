## 6. Two judgements the Orchestrator asked for

- Are the shim's NMI emulation and the nmi_internal rule intent-derived? Yes for their semantics, with one tuned constant. The design document doc/03_reference/exception_interrupts.rst is the intent: the NMI has id 31 and vectors to mtvec base + 0x7C (:57), it is enabled independent of mstatus / mie and not visible in mip (:56), all interrupts including the NMI are ignored while handling it and nested NMIs are not supported (:58-59, :178-179), an internal interrupt produces an NMI on the same vector with mcause and mtval set to its cause (:72), the external NMI outranks the internal ones (:73), the load integrity error's mcause is 0xFFFFFFE0 and the external NMI's 0x8000001F (:84, :90), and the mstack CSRs back up mstatus.MPP / MPIE, mepc and mcause for a recoverable NMI (:93-96, :176). The shim implements exactly these (gen_isa_shim.cc:437-451, :486-493, :624-628) and the checker's acceptance of an NMI-vector entry rests on the driver's own announcement, never on the DUT's self-report; the RTL (rtl/ibex_controller.sv:391-430) is cited only for the pending-bit mechanics the document leaves implicit (one bit, set by any data-response integrity error, cleared by the entry, the first corruption's address as mtval), which is a permissible internal anchor for a quantity intent does not fix. Not intent-derived: the latency bound of 4 records, documented as "observed 2-3" (tuned from the DUT), and the checker keeps counting inside debug mode although handle_irq (:498) excludes it; state the bound as an upper margin over the RTL-derived latency (the pending bit is taken at the next decode opportunity outside NMI and debug mode) and suspend the count in debug mode (adopted L-7).
- Do 448 undecidable claims (78 percent of 573) change my LOG-037b credit condition? The condition stands ("an item whose fire check is the priority pick is credited from a run where its entry's claim was decidable") and its meaning sharpens: under the storm with the taken-line release almost no entry is decidable, so priority items cannot be credited from the storm at all; they are credited from directed or sparse cases where the contending lines do not move inside the window (gen_ut_irq's two-entry loop, fast14 + fast0 both held until taken, is exactly such a case and is decidable). Two consequences for tb-infra and the DV Lead: the cause of the rise from 40 to 78 percent must be stated (L-8; the multi-line storm, not the NMI pulses of I-2), and the checker should publish per-entry decidability (a bridge event or a per-test count the fire check can read) so that a priority item's fire check can assert its own entry was decidable instead of the plan relying on a run-level count. Soundness is unchanged: rule (i) applies to every entry the checker sees; rule (ii) is degraded, not wrong; the entries the checker does not see are H-1.
